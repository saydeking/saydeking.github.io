I’m proud to share that our paper, **“Evaluating Visual and Behavioral Signals of Deception in Real-World Contexts,”** received the Best Paper Award at SBP-BRiMS 2025. I coauthored the work with Hoorad Abootalebi, Georgia Ng Wai, and Tempestt Neal.

The study examined visual and behavioral signals used in deception detection across biographical, academic, well-being, and crime-related topics. The usefulness of cues and model performance varied across these contexts. That variation is central to the contribution: evaluating a model in one setting does not tell us how its signals will perform in another. Context needs to be part of how we assess these systems and interpret their outputs.

This recognition is especially meaningful because Georgia’s contributions grew out of her undergraduate research. I’m grateful to my coauthors and collaborators for the care and expertise they brought to this work, and to the SBP-BRiMS community for recognizing it.
